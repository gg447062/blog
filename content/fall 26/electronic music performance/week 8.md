listening

mario vega at dark circuits festival 2014

using a taxidermy goat's foot as a controller goes incredibly hard. the performance starts with droney, whispery textures, and then some eerie voice sounds. he's great a using dynamics and space, building up sounds and then cutting them almost to silence. great use of textures and frequencies as well, some buzzy, some metallic some whining high pitches and some deeper droney ones. he also puts a lot of his body into the movements of the controls of his instruments. he really makes a knob turning very dramatic, which is quite engaging. i like how much time he takes to not touch anything and just stand and wait and listen. i admire that kind of patience, and in terms of performance, it gives him an interesting relationship to his instruments, like he's watching it to see what it will do next. 

joker nies and miguel frasconi at dark circuits fesival

this performance starts out super bubbly and aquatic sounding. this one was cool to see as a live performance because it showed what tools they were using for their performance. i noticed the two omnichords of course, plus a ciat lonbarde synth and a bunch of pedals. i kind of like it when i don't know how a particular sound is made, since it promotes an acousmatic listening approach, but sometimes it's nice to see what tools people use as a reference. it was cool to see the omnichord used in a more dissonant and atonal way. it's usually so cute and pretty. there were lots of beeps and boops and high pitched squeals as well as a litltle bit of drum machine making an appearance at the end.

yasunao tone live @ ctm09 berlin

another set by the glitch goat. it's so lovely to see him dressed like a professor in a suit and tie making an ungodly electronic racket, at probably unimaginable volumes. this one is incredibly dense, a wall of electronic chirps, zaps and buzzes all happening at once. many of them are rhythmic and it creates many overlapping counter rhythms that confuse the ear. he's good at moving the piece from chaotic and dense to static and more sparse though, allowing a moment of relative calm static before setting everything back to full chaos. it's hard to tell from the recording and just my computer speakers, but it sounds like there's a lot of high to mid range frequencies playing, with not as much low end. ear piercing.
