listening response

yasunao tone - a radio music (1963)

i was curious to listen to this one because all i've heard from tone is his work with cds. this piece was a performance lasting about 2 minutes. the performer entered with a suitcase and proceeded to place it on the floor and open it to reveal a collection of portable radios within. he then pulled them out one by one seemingly at random and appeared to adjust the tuning and volume of the different radios as he went. after doing this for a couple of minutes he return the radio he was holding to the case, closed it, and walked out. 

i thought this was very relevant to our class discussions of composition techniques. this felt very much like one of cage's indeterminate pieces. one could imagine the instructions for this one easily, and because the source material depends on the radio broadcasts occurring within the duration of the piece, it is guaranteed to be different every time.

michael lowenstern - spasm

this one was great. it's described as 'contemporary classical bass clarinet & electronics' on the site. i loved the display of the wide timbral range of the bass clarinet: at times it was hard to tell which sounds were the electronics and which were the clarinet. 

to me it felt like the performance fell closer to the karaoke dynamic than a fully live piece, but i could be wrong. it seemed like there could maybe have been some times where the clarinet parts were used as triggers to transition to another section in the piece. 

rajesh mehta - drumpet

this is a video of mehta playing a trumpet with a piece of tubing attached to it. somehow this piece of tubing is making super percussive almost metallic sounds. definitely sounds electronically generated but it isn't. i could have watched a full set of this alone. I like that this one has no electronics at all but still offers the range of sounds that something electrified might be expected to create. it also helped that he was playing it in a room with a ton of reverb which made it sound awesome.

this instrument works really well for a live performance because of the gestural possibilities it offers. the performance showed a nice balance in terms of volume dynamics as well as textural dynamics. i liked that he started more sparse and used the empty spaces in between sounds to build up the tension. the reverb in the room worked really well in the quiet parts and also added a nice bed of chaos when he started playing more dense bursts of sound.
