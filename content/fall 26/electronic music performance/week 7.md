
listening response

phantom orchard: ikue more and zeena parkins. 
very cool harp playing, not used to seeing the harp played in experimental ways/settings. i love the super loose and buzzy string. the electronics are so watery and bubbly but at the same time buzzy and electric. they play together really well, the way that they work with volume dynamics and the sparse/dense spectrum is very inspiring, they flow so naturally. i always love the combination of electronics and acoustic or electroacoustic instruments. in this case they compliment each other particularly well. 

john zorn, cobra. 
featuring the legend nicolas collins on the trombone powered electronics! i love the idea of a game as a version of an alternative score. it's hard to tell exactly what is going on here, but it's cool to see such exaggerated gestural interaction happening in a live music performance. in all performance but especially improvised performance, listening and paying attention to the other performers is paramount to the effective execution of the piece, but it is usually relatively subtle, limited to a glance or a head nod. but here the performers gesture wildly and strangely to each other communicating in ways that are still quite obscure to the audience. the music itself is chaotic and wild, with frenetic drums and a ton of samples.

chris mann (music for making sense) - the act of observations becomes the object itself. lovely textures and timbres. a lot of small chittery sounds and whooshing sounds that pan left and right. seems like heavy use of sampling, mostly textured sounds but occasionally sounds of voices and instruments like basses, strings, and synthesizers. the video i watched seems like a 12 minute series of vignettes that last for a few seconds or a minute. very abstract and cinematic, more of a sketch of a feeling or a moment than a 'song' per se
