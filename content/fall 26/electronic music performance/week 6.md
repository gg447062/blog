john oswald reading response

these are mostly impressions jotted down as i read.

my first impression of this essay is that oswald has a great sense of humor. now knowing of his infamy as a 'robber' of music and degenerate misuser of michael jackson's image, his mischievous persona stands out through his humor. 

his description of a sampler as both a "documenting device and a creative device" is beautiful that reminds me of that way that 80s fanzines used the photocopier as a creative device although it's original intention was essentially for documentation. 

there's also some nice recording industry history here. i was unaware that at the time this piece was written, the recording industry was threatened by blank tapes and people dubbing cassettes of music they liked. i find this to be quite funny given that throughout the immense popularity of the mixtape and it's younger sibling the mix cd, the record industry thrived. not until the rise of the mp3 did it's star begin to fall.

i love the idea of a musical quotation mark, i had never thought about the power of the written one, or how all that stands between a reference and plagiarism are a pair of two apostrophes. 

at the end of the section titled "the commerce of noise" he hits the idea of AI generated music dead on. and in some ways predicts such musical aids as the 'scale' function in the ableton midi piano roll.

glad to see harry partch mentioned!

i tend to agree with oswald that once popular music become part of the sound ecology of a place, it naturally should fall into the category of public domain, the way that the sound of the subway or a semi truck honking are free from copyright. i also agree that we should be able to wield the technology we have to both document and create with the raw materials of the sounds around us, music, noise, or otherwise.
