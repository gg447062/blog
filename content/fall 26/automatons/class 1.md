![[IMG_3798.mov]]

when i first got this motorcycle toy, i thought it was going to use some kind of ratcheting mechanism where you wind it by dragging it backwards before letting go, but it turns out this one works differently. Instead of storing and releasing energy, this one seems to use momentum to prolong and increase the amount of force the user inputs, in the same direction they input it. so the harder you 'prime' it by rolling it forward, the faster it will roll and the further it will go.

In addition to the two normal motorcycle wheels, on the bottom there are two smaller wheels with a ridged surface on the circumference. these wheels have some resistance when they are spun, and once they are moving they have some 'weight' to them and require some force to stop. this in addition to the fact that they make a whining sound when they spin that leads me to think they are connected to some gears and bearings and maybe a flywheel. in all honesty, i don't actually know how a flywheel works or what exactly it is, but i just saw one described in a video about how a combustion engine's pistons work. the way that motorcycle's second pair of wheels continue to spin after the initial force that caused them to spin has dissipated reminds me of the way the flywheel conserves and prolongs the motion of the piston after the fuel has combusted. thus it seems like a possible candidate for inclusion in the toy motorcycle's mechanism.

![[class 1-1789433433264.webp]]

After disassembling my toy, I learned that my intuition was correct! It did in fact use a gear system along with a flywheel as the mechanism. I didn't see any visible bearings but there was some sort of lubricant speckled on the bottom of the mechanism's container so i would assume that it played the part of the bearings. I was interested to see that the majority of the parts in this toy had nothing to do with the mechanism and were instead just to make it look cool.
