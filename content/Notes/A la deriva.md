**LOGAN**:

add some grain: toss an adjustment layer over the top of the whole thing and add the effect "add grain". set that thing to a film grain you like and turn the intensity down a bit so it's super subtle. all cameras add grain so if you ever want something to feel less digital, just add some grain/noise. definitely works best if its super subtle almost unnoticeable

add some jitter using an expression for a more stop motion style effect: add a null layer or an empty shape layer. open up the position property of that layer, hold down the 'alt' key and click the stopwatch of the position property. type: wiggle(6,2). click out of this to exit editing the expression. now parent all your layers (except the background) to the null layer with the expression. you should see stuff randomly wiggling around. u can adjust the amount of the expression by changing the numbers: (6= how many times it moves per second, 2= how much it moves per second). there's a sweet spot and often times the more subtle the better. alternatively you could put this expression on each of you layers to really give it a sketchy cutout feel. to take this further you can put an adjustment layer over the whole thing with a "posterize time" effect. lower this number to 12, 8 or 6 and this will change the frame rate of everything giving it a stop motion look

overlaying some textures is another approach and this guy has some useful tutorials on the topic: [https://youtu.be/CqCbwMbidBw?si=DxRRddraJGXEdIK5](https://youtu.be/CqCbwMbidBw?si=DxRRddraJGXEdIK5)
