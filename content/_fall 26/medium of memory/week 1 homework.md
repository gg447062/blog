### first memory
one of my first memories is of the basement in the house where i lived for the first few years of my life. the room is illuminated by the cozy light of a lamp behind me to my right. where i'm sitting there is a couch and a tv and the wall to my left. to my right in front of me are the stairs going up to the kitchen and beyond that is a nebulous area that i don't remember well but i have a vague sensation that it was an open carpeted area not too much bigger than the section i am in. 

 this is where i watch movies, either on my own or with my family. right now we are watching pocahontas and the scene where she sings colors of the wind is playing. i feel excited by the movie and at the same time relaxed and at ease because of the coziness of the room and the comfort of watching a movie with my family.

### media reflections

*where do children's earliest memories go?*

**"I intended to jostle out a recalcitrant memory with the sights, sounds, smells and touch of the place."**
i like that the author mentions getting on her knees to examine the cabin from a toddler's point of view in order to try and trigger old memories



**"they don’t have a greatly elaborated sense of self, which would encourage them to hoard and reconsider chunks of experience as part of a growing life-narrative."**

memories and identity feed back on each other? like one creates the other ouroboros style? sense of self encourages remembering things and memories help create a sense of self


**"memory shredding"**

**"Our memories can become distorted by other people’s memories of the same event"**

i think this happens as an adult as well. 

 **"If you never use that memory, those synapses can be recruited for something different"**
 
 **"the mother (or another adult) engages the child in a lively conversation about events, always passing the baton of remembering back to the child and inviting him or her to contribute to the story"**

 so remembering is kind of like a muscle, you have to use it or you lose it. I wonder what the effects of constant image and video making have on this aspect of memory? are people relying more on those kinds of 'objective' exterior memories instead of their own memory.

**"'the reminiscence bump' from ages 15 to 30"** 

**"The events, culture and people of that time remain with us and can even overshadow the features of our ageing present, according to Bauer. The movies were the best back then, and so was the music, and the fashion, and the political leaders, and the friendships, and the romances"**

I think this part is the most interesting to me, even though the article is mostly about early memories, because this phenomenon always felt like an obstructive nostalgia to me, like this kind of glorifying of the past blocks people from living in the present. it speaks also to the subjective nature of memory
